# Currency imagery — attribution and licensing

**Please read before shipping.** The *designs* of Indian banknotes and coins are the copyright of the
Reserve Bank of India and the Government of India, and that copyright is not waived by anything below.
What is licensed here is the *photograph or scan* of each note and coin, sourced from Wikimedia Commons
and recorded verbatim-ish from each Commons file page. These assets are used in MindMitra as an
educational / assistive aid in a non-commercial prototype, to help a person living with dementia
recognise the money she actually handles. That use is a deliberate, product-owner-accepted judgement
call in a legal grey area — it is not a cleared commercial licence. Anyone shipping this app
commercially should obtain their own cleared currency imagery from RBI, or simply photograph their own
notes and coins and replace every file in this directory. Note also that reproducing current banknote
imagery is separately regulated as an anti-counterfeiting matter in most jurisdictions, independent of
copyright; several images below are RBI's own SPECIMEN-overprinted publications, which is the safer
form to reproduce.

## Honest notes on each image

- **No image here is non-free or fair-use.** Every file is tagged with an open licence on Commons
  (GODL-India or CC BY-SA 4.0). Non-free Commons currency images were found during sourcing and were
  deliberately not used.
- **SPECIMEN overprints.** `note-10`, `note-50`, `note-100` and `note-500` are RBI's own specimen
  publications and carry a red diagonal **SPECIMEN** across the right-hand watermark panel, plus
  `000000` serial numbers. Commons alternatives without the overprint were checked and rejected: they
  were either reverse faces, older pre-2018 designs, third-party scans carrying CamScanner-style
  watermarks, or annotated two-face collages. The colour, Gandhi portrait and large denomination
  numeral all remain clearly legible, which is what the game needs. `note-20` has no overprint.
- **Coins show the denomination face, not the numismatic obverse.** On Indian coins the obverse is the
  Ashoka Lion Capital side, which carries *no* value marking at all and is therefore useless for a
  numeracy task. Every coin image here is the face bearing the numeral, which is what the player must
  read. Where a Commons filename says "Obverse" (₹10) it in fact depicts the numeral face.
- **`coin-1.jpg` is soft.** The only current-design (2011 rupee-symbol series) ₹1 denomination face on
  Commons is 126×124px; it has been upscaled to 420px. Sharper ₹1 photographs exist on Commons but all
  show the superseded pre-2011 wheat-ear design, which an elder may no longer recognise — so currency
  of design was preferred over sharpness. This is the weakest asset in the set and the first one worth
  replacing with an original photograph.
- **CC BY-SA 4.0 is share-alike.** The four coin images carry copyleft obligations. If that is
  unacceptable downstream, the coins are the files to re-shoot.

## Processing applied

All files were downloaded via the Wikimedia Commons API, converted to progressive JPEG, flattened onto
white where the source PNG had transparency, and resized to roughly 420–650px on the long edge at
quality 90. No cropping, retouching, or removal of overprints was performed — the images are unaltered
in content.
